Your Pitcher Is a Fielder Too: Pitch Counts, Positions and the Pitcher's Defensive Jobs

What a youth pitcher does after the pitch (covering first, backing up bases, covering home) and how to manage pitch counts and positions on game day with two free apps.

5 min read · September 24, 2026

Every youth coach knows the pitcher's pitch count matters. League rules cap it, rest days depend on it, and somebody in the dugout is tracking every pitch. But the moment the ball is hit, your pitcher becomes the busiest fielder on the diamond, and that part usually gets far less practice.

The pitcher's four defensive jobs

1. Cover first on balls to the right side

Any ground ball to the left of the mound, the pitcher breaks toward 1st: get to the baseline a few steps short of the bag, run up the inside of the line (the fair side, out of the runner's lane), and take the toss on the inside of the bag. Running straight at the base, or up the foul side, leads to collisions with the runner.

Softball: many fastpitch teams have the second baseman cover 1st whenever the first baseman leaves the bag, and the pitcher backs it up. Ask your coach which your team does.

Watch this play
Ground ball to the right side. The pitcher breaks to 1st: to the line, then up the line to the bag.

2. Field comebackers and bunts

Balls hit back at the mound are the pitcher's. With a runner on 1st and less than two outs, turn and throw to 2nd to start the double play.

Watch this play
Comebacker with a runner on 1st. The pitcher turns and throws to 2nd to start the double play.

3. Back up throws from the outfield

On a hit to the outfield, the pitcher backs up the base the throw is going to: behind 3rd, or behind the catcher on a throw home. If it isn't clear yet, go halfway between 3rd and home and read the throw.

Watch this play
Single to right with a runner on 2nd. The pitcher backs up home, behind the catcher.

4. Cover home on passed balls

When a pitch gets past the catcher with a runner on 3rd, the pitcher sprints to home plate, points to the ball, and yells so the catcher knows where to throw. Set up in front of the plate, not on it: leave the runner a path to the back of the plate, catch the ball, then sweep the tag down.

Tired arms, slow feet

Pitch count limits exist to protect young arms, and a pitcher who is near the limit is usually slower getting off the mound too. That's one more reason to keep a close eye on the count, and to think about the defensive jobs when you plan who pitches when. Keep the rest-day rules in mind when you move a pitcher to catcher or another position later in the game. Many leagues limit that too.

Quick answers

What does the pitcher do after the ball is hit?
The pitcher becomes a fielder: fields comebackers and bunts, covers 1st on balls hit to the right side, backs up 3rd or home on throws from the outfield, and covers home on passed balls and wild pitches.
Why does the pitcher cover first base?
On a ground ball to the first baseman, nobody else can get to the bag in time. The pitcher runs to the baseline, then up the line, and takes the toss on the bag.
