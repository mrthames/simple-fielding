First Base Responsibilities: Where the First Baseman Goes on Every Play

Where the first baseman goes on every play: getting to the bag on ground balls, holding runners, charging bunts, and being the cutoff on throws home from center and right. For youth baseball and softball.

On most ground balls the first baseman's job is simple and important: get to the bag, give the thrower a target, and catch it. More outs are made at first than anywhere else.

On hits to the outfield, the first baseman has a bigger job than most kids expect: in a common youth system, they're the cutoff on throws home from center and right field.

The first baseman's main jobs

  • On a ground ball to another infielder, get to the bag, set your feet, and stretch toward the throw once you see where it is.
  • On a bunt, charge in. The second baseman covers first behind you.
  • On a single to center or right with a runner trying to score, be the cutoff for home: line up between the ball and the plate, arms up, and listen for the catcher.
  • With a runner on first where leadoffs are allowed (50/70 and up, softball), hold the runner on: foot on the bag, glove up as a target.

Quick answers

Who is the cutoff on a throw home from center field?
In a common youth system, the first baseman is the cutoff on throws home from center and right field, and the third baseman from left field. Some teams use the pitcher instead, so check your team's system.
Who covers first base when the first baseman charges a bunt?
The second baseman. On a bunt, the first baseman charges in and the second baseman runs to cover first.
Should the first baseman stretch before the throw?
No. Get to the bag and set your feet first. Stretch toward the throw once you can see where it's going, so you don't commit the wrong way.

Where teams do it differently (who's the cutoff home, who covers second on a steal), these pages follow a common youth system. Your coach's system wins.
