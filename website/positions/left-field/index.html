Left Field Responsibilities: Where the Left Fielder Goes on Every Play

Where the left fielder goes on every play: charging ground balls, hitting the cutoff, and backing up third base and the infield. For youth baseball and softball.

In youth baseball, more balls are hit to left field than anywhere else in the outfield, because most young hitters are right-handed and pull the ball.

When the ball isn't hit to them, the left fielder is still moving: backing up third, or backing up the infielder on their side.

The left fielder's main jobs

  • On a hit to left, charge the ball, get it, and throw it to the cutoff chest-high. Don't hold it.
  • On a ground ball to the left side of the infield, charge in to back up the fielder.
  • On a throw to third (a steal of third, a runner going first to third), back up third base.
  • Let the center fielder take fly balls between you: the center fielder has priority.

Quick answers

Where does the left fielder throw the ball?
To the cutoff, usually the shortstop or the third baseman, chest-high. Throwing through the cutoff gets the ball back to the infield fastest, and the cutoff can redirect it.
Who backs up third base?
The left fielder, on throws to third. On a throw home, the pitcher backs up the catcher.
Who takes a fly ball between left and center field?
The center fielder has priority over the corner outfielders. The first clear call wins; if the center fielder calls it, the left fielder backs them up.

Where teams do it differently (who's the cutoff home, who covers second on a steal), these pages follow a common youth system. Your coach's system wins.
