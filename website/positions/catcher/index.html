Catcher Responsibilities on Defense: Where the Catcher Goes on Every Play

The catcher sees the whole field: backing up first on ground balls, calling cutoffs, covering home, and directing bunt plays. Here's every play for youth baseball and softball catchers.

The catcher is the only player facing the whole field, so the catcher is the defense's eyes and voice: calling where the throw goes, telling the cutoff what to do, and taking charge of plays at the plate.

When nobody is on base, the youth catcher has one more job that's easy to forget: run down the line behind the batter to back up first.

The catcher's main jobs

  • On a ground ball with nobody on (or nobody on second or third), run down the first-base line, in foul territory, to back up the throw to first.
  • With a runner who might score, stay home. Set up in front of the plate, and call the cutoff: say nothing to let the throw through, "Cut!" to have it caught, "Cut two!" to have it thrown to second.
  • On a bunt, call who takes it and where the throw goes. The catcher sees it best.
  • On a pop-up behind the plate, turn your back to the field, find the ball, then toss the mask away from where you're going.

Quick answers

Does the catcher back up first base?
In youth baseball, yes: on a ground ball to the infield when there's nobody on second or third, the catcher runs down the line in foul territory behind the batter to back up the throw to first.
What does the catcher yell to the cutoff?
Nothing if the throw should go through to the plate. "Cut!" to have the cutoff catch it and hold it. "Cut two!" (or three) to have the cutoff catch it and throw to that base, usually to get a runner who took an extra base.
Who calls where the ball goes on a bunt?
The catcher, because the catcher is facing the play and can see the runners. The fielder picks up the ball and throws where the catcher calls.

Where teams do it differently (who's the cutoff home, who covers second on a steal), these pages follow a common youth system. Your coach's system wins.
