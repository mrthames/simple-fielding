Shortstop Responsibilities: Where the Shortstop Goes on Every Play

Where the shortstop goes on every play: fielding the most ground balls, covering second, going out as the relay on balls to left and center, and leading the infield. For youth baseball and softball.

The shortstop gets more ground balls than anyone and has the longest throw across the infield. They're also the infield's leader: saying the outs, reminding everyone where the play is, and taking charge on pop-ups.

When the ball isn't hit to them, the shortstop is almost always going somewhere: covering second or third, or out into the outfield as the relay.

The shortstop's main jobs

  • Field ground balls on the left side, and take the lead runner when there's time.
  • On a ground ball to the second baseman with a runner on first, cover second for the force.
  • On a deep ball to left or left-center, go out as the relay, in line with the throw; on a ball to right, trail behind the second baseman.
  • Before every pitch, say the outs and where the play is. On pop-ups between infielders, the shortstop has priority over the corners.

Quick answers

What does the shortstop do on a single to left field?
In a common youth system, the shortstop goes out as the cutoff toward left field, lined up between the ball and second base, so the left fielder has a close target. The throw comes back in through them.
Who covers second base on a ground ball to the second baseman?
The shortstop. With a runner on first, the second baseman throws to the shortstop covering the bag for the force.
Why is the shortstop the infield leader?
The shortstop can see the whole infield and is involved in most plays, so they call the outs before each pitch, remind everyone where the throw goes, and take charge on pop-ups in the middle of the field.

Where teams do it differently (who's the cutoff home, who covers second on a steal), these pages follow a common youth system. Your coach's system wins.
