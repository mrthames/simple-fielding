Pitcher Responsibilities on Defense: Where the Pitcher Goes on Every Play

After the pitch, the pitcher is a fielder: covering first on balls to the right side, covering home on a passed ball, and backing up bases on hits. Here's every play, from youth baseball and softball.

Once the ball leaves their hand, the pitcher becomes the fifth infielder. The best youth pitchers know that throwing strikes is only half the job.

The pitcher's defensive work comes down to three habits: field what's hit back to you, run to first on anything to the right side, and back up the base the throw is going to.

The pitcher's main jobs

  • Field comebackers and bunts in front of the mound, then throw to the base the catcher calls.
  • On a ground ball to the right side, run toward first right away. If the first baseman fields it, cover the base: run to a spot a few steps up the line, then turn and run along it to the bag.
  • On a passed ball or wild pitch with a runner on third, sprint in and cover home.
  • On a hit to the outfield, back up the base the throw is going to: third, or behind the catcher on a throw home.

Quick answers

Does the pitcher cover first base?
Yes, on any ball hit to the right side of the infield. The pitcher breaks toward first on contact. If the first baseman fields the ball, the pitcher covers the bag and takes the throw.
Where does the pitcher go on a hit to the outfield?
Behind the base the throw is going to. On a throw home, the pitcher backs up the catcher. On a throw to third, the pitcher backs up third. If it isn't clear yet, go halfway between third and home and read the throw.
Who covers home on a passed ball?
The pitcher. The catcher chases the ball and the pitcher sprints in to cover the plate.

Where teams do it differently (who's the cutoff home, who covers second on a steal), these pages follow a common youth system. Your coach's system wins.
