Most youth practices spend plenty of time on catching and throwing, and very little on where to be. Then game day comes, a ball gets past the shortstop, and four kids stand still watching it. Here's a 30-minute plan that fixes that, and it works for 8-year-olds or 12-year-olds, baseball or softball.

What you need

  • Your full team, in their positions, plus a coach with a bat or a bucket of balls. Extra players are baserunners, in helmets, so the fielders have real runners to play against. Swap them in every few reps.
  • Something to show the play on: a whiteboard, a clipboard, or a tablet. Simple Fielding is free and animates every player, so kids can watch the whole play before they run it.
  • Cones or throw-down bases to mark spots, if you have them.

0:00–5:00 · Show it (whiteboard)

Pick two plays for the day. Not ten. Show each one and stop at the moment the ball is fielded: "Freeze. Where is everybody? Why?" Then let it play out. Kids remember a picture of the field far better than a list of rules.

Good first pairs:

  • Grounder to 1st (pitcher covers) + single to left (cutoff)
  • Double play + pop-up priority
  • Bunt defense + passed ball with a runner on 3rd

5:00–15:00 · Walk it (half speed)

Everyone in position. Coach calls the situation ("Runner on first, one out") and points to where the ball is hit. Nobody throws. Every player walks to their spot and says their job out loud: "Cover second!" "Back up first!" Check each spot, then reset.

Rotate every few reps so kids see the play from more than one position. The right fielder who has once stood at second base understands why the throw goes there.

15:00–25:00 · Run it (live)

Now a coach hits or rolls the ball, and it's live. Use the freeze call: after the ball is fielded, yell "Freeze!" and every player stops where they are. Walk around and check. Praise the backups loudly, because they're the ones nobody notices during games.

25:00–30:00 · Quiz and wrap up

Put a situation on the screen and ask one player at a time: "Ball hit here, runner on first. Where do you go?" Let them come up and drag their own position on the whiteboard. Finish with one fun live rep.

Tips from the dugout

  • Two plays a practice. Stack them week to week; don't try to cover everything at once.
  • Use their names. "Maya, you're the cutoff" lands better than "shortstop." In Simple Fielding you can put your players' names on the field.
  • Repeat before games. Five minutes on a phone or tablet before warm-ups is a great refresher.
  • Projector for team meetings. Projector mode fills the screen with just the field.
