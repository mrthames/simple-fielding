Softball runners leave the base when the pitch is released, so after every pitch there's a runner standing off the bag. Without a rule, they could dance off the base all day. The look-back rule ends that: once the pitcher has the ball in the circle, the runner has to decide.

What the rule says

When the pitcher has the ball in the eight-foot pitching circle and isn't making a play, a runner who is off the base must immediately go back to the base or go on to the next one. If she stops, or starts one way and then changes direction, she's out. The umpire calls it; nobody has to throw.

The exact wording varies by rulebook. USA Softball and NFHS (high school) let the runner stop once. NCAA (college) doesn't allow a stop at all. Check your league.

What the defense does

The whole defense has one job after a pitch: get the ball to the circle. The catcher throws it right back to the pitcher. The pitcher gets in the circle, faces the runner, and holds the ball. Don't fake a throw: in college a fake frees the runner, and at any level it gives her a reason to keep going.

The third baseman gets to the bag in case the pitcher needs to throw.

When she stops

If the runner freezes off the base with the ball in the circle, don't throw. She's out by rule.

When she breaks for home

If the runner on third breaks as the pitcher gets the ball, the pitcher throws home right away, and the catcher, who got right back to the plate after throwing, makes the tag. The shortstop backs up third in case she turns back into a rundown.

Teaching it

For young players the rule is simpler than it sounds: the play isn't over until the ball is in the circle. Outfielders throw it in, infielders get it to the pitcher, and the pitcher holds it facing the runner.
