Runner on first, the pitch is on its way, and the runner takes off. The catcher comes up throwing, and two infielders run to second base at the same time. Or worse, neither does. Here's the rule that settles it.

The rule: cover from the side the batter won't hit to

Batters usually pull the ball: right-handed hitters to the left side, lefties to the right side. So the infielder on the pull side stays home in case the ball is put in play, and the other one covers.

Many teams make it even simpler: the shortstop and second baseman decide before the pitch. One of them shows the other a closed mouth (me) or an open mouth (you) behind the glove. Whatever the system, they talk before every pitch with a runner on.

Everybody else

  • Center fielder: charges in behind 2nd to back up the throw. This saves more runs than people think.
  • Pitcher: after the pitch, gets out of the way. Duck or step aside so the catcher has a clear lane.
  • Catcher: catches, comes up throwing, and aims at the bag, knee high, so the tag is quick.
  • Covering infielder: straddles the bag, catches the ball, and puts the tag down in front of the base. Let the runner slide into the tag.

Stealing 3rd

Much simpler: the third baseman covers, the shortstop covers 2nd behind the runner, and the left fielder charges in to back up the throw. A bad throw to 3rd without a backup is a run.

1st & 3rd: the double steal

This is where teams differ most. The runner on 1st goes, and if the catcher throws to 2nd, the runner on 3rd breaks for home. Options include throwing through, faking to 3rd, throwing back to the pitcher, or holding the ball. One common youth version is the cut play: the second baseman comes in front of the bag, the shortstop covers 2nd, and the third baseman yells "Four!" if the runner breaks for home. The second baseman catches it and throws home.

A note on leagues

In Little League baseball on a 60-foot field, runners can't leave the base until the pitch reaches the batter, so steals are rarer and the throw is shorter. In fastpitch softball, runners can go when the pitcher releases the ball. In 50/70, travel ball and older divisions with leadoffs, pickoffs and pitcher holds come into play too. Simple Fielding has a setting for each, and it changes when runners start moving.
