Strike three, the batter walks away, and then someone yells "Run!" The catcher didn't catch it cleanly, and now the batter is sprinting to first. The dropped third strike is one of the most confusing rules for young players. It's actually simple once you know the two conditions.

When the batter can run

On strike three that the catcher doesn't catch cleanly, the batter can run to first if:

  • First base is open, or
  • There are two outs.

If first base is occupied and there are fewer than two outs, the batter is out, caught or not. The rule exists so the catcher can't drop the ball on purpose to start a double play.

Younger divisions often skip the rule: in Little League Minors, the batter is simply out. It applies in Little League Majors and up. In softball it varies by league at 10U and 12U.

What the catcher and first baseman do

The catcher gets the ball and throws to first. The key is the throwing lane: the batter is running right down the line, and a throw that hits them is a disaster. So the first baseman calls the side:

  • Ball in front of the plate: the first baseman sets up on the inside (fair side) of the bag and yells "Inside!". The catcher steps into fair territory and throws.
  • Ball at the backstop: the first baseman sets up on the outside (foul side) and yells "Outside!".

The second baseman cheats toward first to back up the throw, and the right fielder charges in behind first. The pitcher gets out of the catcher's throwing lane.

Bases loaded, two outs

With the bases loaded and two outs, every runner is forced, including a runner coming home. The catcher doesn't need to throw at all: pick up the ball and step on home plate for the third out.

When not to throw

Runner on first and one out? The batter is already out. A throw to first is a wasted throw that can go into right field. Pick up the ball and watch the runners.
