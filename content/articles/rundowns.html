A runner is caught between bases. The defense has them trapped, and all it has to do is tag them. Then three throws later, the ball is rolling toward the fence and the runner is standing on third.

Rundowns go wrong when they turn into a game of catch. The fix is a few simple rules every infielder can learn.

The rules

  1. Run them back. Chase the runner toward the base they came from. If anything goes wrong, they're back where they started, not a base ahead.
  2. Ball up. Hold the ball up by your ear where your teammate can see it, and run hard at the runner. Make them commit.
  3. One throw. The fielder at the base steps up toward the runner with their glove up and yells "Now!" when the runner is close. One firm, chest-high throw, and the tag.
  4. No fakes. Fake throws fool your own teammate more than the runner.
  5. Get out of the lane. After you throw, step out of the base path. A fielder in the runner's way without the ball is obstruction, and the runner gets the base.

Who backs up

Two players back up every rundown, one behind each base, in case the ball gets away or the runner turns around. On a rundown between third and home, the pitcher backs up home and the shortstop backs up third.

Practicing it

Rundowns are easy to drill: two fielders, one runner, two bases. Have the fielders say "Now!" out loud every time, and count the throws. Two throws is good. One is great.
